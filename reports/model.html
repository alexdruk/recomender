Ranking quality: take one book I like (my library, or scored 4–5) and one book I do not (background, or scored 1–2); the number says how often the model puts the right one higher. 50% is guessing, 100% is perfect. Measured on authors the model had not seen.

What the model can and cannot do

  • It can sort new books so that books like the ones in my library come first: put one book I like and one ordinary book side by side, and it picks the right one about 83 times in 100 when the book has reader votes and 77 times in 100 when it has none (50 would be guessing).
  • It can score a brand-new book that nobody has rated: such a book sits at the catalog average and is judged by its text, style, genre and the record of its author and series, and it is scored again when votes arrive.
  • It cannot yet tell a book I would score 5 from one I would score 3: it has only 8 of my scores, so today it mostly knows "looks like my library" from "looks ordinary", and its predicted scores stay near the middle; this is measured from 30 scores on.

2,183 of these books have reader votes, 2,383 have an author record, 1,202 a series record. For a random half (1,457 books) the book's own rating was hidden in training, so the model also learns to score books without votes. The catalog average for fiction is 3.38, from 2,287,467 votes on 293,933 books; a book without votes sits exactly there. Books that wait in "To send" or "Reading" without a score are left out.

Before a book is scored, a separate text check (fiction against non-fiction) looks at it: trained on 2861 fiction and 93 non-fiction books, accuracy 98.1% in an author-grouped test; it catches 65 of 93 non-fiction books and wrongly sets aside 29 of 2861 fiction books.

Factors it does not use

  • the year the book was written or added
  • the text of reader comments and reviews, and how many there are
  • the annotation and the cover
  • the theme tags and the summary written by the reading model (they are shown on the card only)
  • the name of the author as such (only the marks of the author's earlier books)
  • the marks of single readers who like the same books as I do
  • the publisher, the translator's name, the file size
  • how often a book is downloaded

Does the reading model improve the ranking?

Yes: the ratings of the reading model improve the ranking by more than chance can explain, for rated books and for books without votes.

Measured on the 761 training books that the reading model (qwen3.5:9b) has read (294 library, 459 background, 8 scored), in the same author-grouped test. The long run is finished: 605 of 605 books read.

"Shuffled ratings" is the control: the same ratings handed to other books, so they carry no information. Whatever it changes is noise. ± is two-sided room for chance, from the spread between the test parts. The reading model counts as helping only when its gain minus twice that room is still above zero and above the control. A background book that the reading model has not read borrows the ratings of a random background book that it has read, so "was read" is never a hint by itself.
