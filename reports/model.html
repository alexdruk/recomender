Ranking quality: take one book I like (my library, or scored 4–5) and one book I do not (background, or scored 1–2); the number says how often the model puts the right one higher. 50% is guessing, 100% is perfect. Measured on authors the model had not seen.

What the model can and cannot do

  • It can sort new books so that books like the ones in my library come first: put one book I like and one ordinary book side by side, and it picks the right one about 82 times in 100 when the book has reader votes and 76 times in 100 when it has none (50 would be guessing).
  • It can score a brand-new book that nobody has rated: such a book sits at the catalog average and is judged by its text, style, genre and the record of its author and series, and it is scored again when votes arrive.
  • It cannot yet tell a book I would score 5 from one I would score 3: it has only 4 of my scores, so today it mostly knows "looks like my library" from "looks ordinary", and its predicted scores stay near the middle; this is measured from 30 scores on.

2,181 of these books have reader votes, 2,347 have an author record, 1,199 a series record. For a random half (1,424 books) the book's own rating was hidden in training, so the model also learns to score books without votes. The catalog average for fiction is 3.38, from 2,287,467 votes on 293,933 books; a book without votes sits exactly there. Books that wait in "To send" or "Reading" without a score are left out.

Before a book is scored, a separate text check (fiction against non-fiction) looks at it: trained on 2794 fiction and 83 non-fiction books, accuracy 98.4% in an author-grouped test; it catches 64 of 83 non-fiction books and wrongly sets aside 28 of 2794 fiction books.

Factors it does not use

  • the year the book was written or added
  • the text of reader comments and reviews, and how many there are
  • the annotation and the cover
  • the name of the author as such (only the marks of the author's earlier books)
  • the marks of single readers who like the same books as I do
  • the publisher, the translator's name, the file size
  • how often a book is downloaded
